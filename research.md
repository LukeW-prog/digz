## Government position

**National Student Accommodation Strategy 2026–2035**, published 24 Mar 2026.
- Target 42,000 beds by 2035.
- **10,000 of those are to come from digs / rent-a-room agreements.**
- No annual targets. Delivery method stated as "a communications strategy"
  and expressions of interest.
- No rent controls, standards or protections for digs.
- Digs sits under DFHERIS's Voluntary Regulatory Framework.

**"Rethink the Room" campaign**, launched 24 Aug 2026 by Minister James
Lawless (Higher Education, and a Kildare TD). Points homeowners to
gov.ie/roomforastudent for a sample agreement. Names no listing platform.

Department-commissioned survey published with it:

## Tax and legal

**Rent-a-Room relief.** Up to €14,000/year tax-free (income tax, PRSI, USC)
for renting a furnished room in your own home. All-or-nothing: go €1 over
and the entire amount becomes taxable. Unchanged for 2026. Revenue updated
its guidance in early 2026 and confirmed it does not clash with the renter's
Rent Tax Credit.

**Rent Tax Credit.** Up to €2,000/year, claimable by the student or their
parents.

**Legal status.** Digs is a licence, not a tenancy. No RTB registration
required, and no RTB protection for the student. Notice periods are whatever
was agreed. Landlord and tenant legislation does not apply.

**Short-term letting register.** Opens 1 Dec 2026, registration deadline
31 Dec 2026, applies to lets of 21 nights or less. Planning restrictions and
a 90-day annual cap proposed for towns and cities over 10,000 population —
Maynooth qualifies. Platforms face fines up to 2% of turnover for listing
unregistered properties.

## Known pain points

Homeowner side: tax confusion and fear of "getting into the system";
safety and privacy; volume of WhatsApp and Daft messages.

Student side: ghosting (reports of 50+ applications with no reply on Daft);
deposit scams in Facebook groups; no RTB protection.

Supply activity peaks May–June. Demand peaks August–September on CAO offers.
Second, smaller demand spike in January for semester two and Erasmus intake.

## Scams and fraud

Garda National Bureau of Criminal Investigation, reported Aug 2026:

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Accommodation scam reports to end July 2026 | 230 |
| Total reported losses | €400,000 |
| Peak season | August–October |

Det Insp Alan Govern noted "notable spikes" in Aug–Oct in each of the last
two years. Three named scam patterns:

1. **The absent landlord.** Claims to be abroad, cannot give access until
   an upfront payment is made.
2. **Mass showings.** Shows the same property to many people, collects a
   deposit from each, disappears.
3. **Fake or unavailable property.** Most common. Property does not exist
   or is not actually for rent. Deposit or holding fee taken, contact ends.

Common thread in all three: **money moves before the student ever sees the
room, and nothing verifies the listing is real or still available.**

Gardaí separately warned that organised crime groups target students via
social media and fake job ads to recruit money mules.

## The search experience today

First-hand audit of daft.ie/student-accommodation-to-share, Sept 2026.
2,722 properties listed nationally.

Filters available: college dropdown, distance radius (0/1/3/5/10/20km),
min and max price, room type (single/double/twin/shared), sort by best
match, most recent, or price.

Filters **not** available:

- Monday–Friday only
- Meals included
- Term-time only dates
- Owner-occupied (i.e. actually digs, not a house share)
- Commute or walk time to campus

**Posting dates are not shown on listings.** There is no indication of
whether a listing is still available.

Reported student experience: one account describes 70 applications on
Daft since late July producing seven viewings and one ghosting.
Rooms in Dublin routinely draw dozens of enquiries within a day.

For comparison, commute-time filtering is standard on Zillow,
Apartments.com and OnTheMarket, by foot, bike, transit or car. No Irish
site offers it.

## Who vets what

**Maynooth University states explicitly:** properties on Maynooth
Studentpad "have not been inspected by the University and the University
does not carry out Garda Vetting on prospective landlords."

**But** MU's international host family programme is different: hosts are
"selected for their friendliness and the standard of their home" and
"all hosts receive a personal visit from an Accommodation Assessor."

So the same university operates two standards in the same town —
assessor-visited host families for international students, and an
unvetted noticeboard for everyone else.

**ISI Dublin** (language school sector) goes further: host families are
visited in advance by their accommodation team and are all Garda vetted.

Nobody applies that standard to ordinary domestic student digs.

## International students

- Standard advice is **not** to book private rental accommodation before
  arriving. Only purpose-built hubs are considered safe to book remotely.
- Students are advised to arrange two weeks of temporary accommodation and
  then view in person.
- Host family digs typically €400–€600/month, usually including weekday
  dinners plus full weekend meals and laundry.
- This is a group that must commit sight-unseen, is told not to, and has
  no trusted remote-booking route for digs.

## Wider rental market

- 7,062 notices of termination in Q1 2026, a 50% rise year on year. Many
  landlords selling rather than accept six-year tenancies under the
  Residential Tenancies Act 2026.
- Third-level student population now cited as **280,000** (earlier figure
  used was ~260,000).

Digs is unaffected by the six-year tenancy rule, because it is a licence.
That is a structural reason digs supply may hold while general rental
supply shrinks.

## Incumbent structural facts

- **Studentpad** is a UK product from Pad Group, ~25 years old, licensed to
  100+ institutions across UK, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand. It is
  generic university accommodation software, not built for Irish digs.
- **Daft** is a generalist property portal. Digs sits inside a 2,722-listing
  share category with no digs-specific handling.
- **myroom.ie** is web only, no app.
- **Digs.ie**, **CollegeCribs.ie** — no app.
- **SpareRoom/Roomgo** — now one company, general flatshare, not digs.

No player in the Irish digs market has a native mobile app.
